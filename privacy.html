Privacy Policy

Voice Vault — last updated 18 October 2026.

Voice Vault is a personal automation agent written and run by Ean Johnson. It has a single user, its author, and runs on a computer in his home. It is not offered to the public, has no sign-up, and collects nothing from anyone else.

What it accesses

With the owner's Google account, and only with his explicit consent, the application uses one Google API scope:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events — to read the day's events so it can discuss the schedule, and to create, amend and delete events so it can set reminders the owner asks for during a call.

It does not request access to Gmail, Drive, Contacts, Photos, location, or any other Google service, and it cannot read calendars other than the owner's own.

What it stores, and where

  • A Google OAuth refresh token, held in a file on the owner's own machine, readable only by the owner's user account.
  • Calendar event titles and times, used during a call and written into the owner's private notes on the same machine.
  • Transcripts of the owner's own phone calls with the agent, kept locally.

None of this is uploaded to a server belonging to the author, because there isn't one.

What it does not do

  • No data is sold, rented, or shared with third parties for advertising or any other purpose.
  • No analytics, trackers, advertising identifiers, or cookies are used. This website itself sets no cookies and runs no scripts.
  • Google user data is never used to train a model.
  • No data is collected from anyone other than the owner.

Third parties

Carrying out a phone call necessarily involves a telephony provider and a speech provider, who process the audio of the call in order to transmit and transcribe it, under their own privacy policies. They receive the call itself; they are not given access to the Google account or to any calendar data beyond what is spoken aloud during the call.

Retention and deletion

Because everything lives on the owner's own machine, the owner deletes it by deleting the files. Google access can be withdrawn at any time from the Google Account permissions page at myaccount.google.com/permissions, which immediately and permanently revokes the application's token.
